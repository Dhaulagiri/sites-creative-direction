# UX foundations research

Researched 2026-10-06. This is a qualitative synthesis of primary research, practitioner guidance, design systems, and accessibility guidance across analytical dashboards, operational monitoring, transactional services, enterprise tools, and native applications. It is not user research on our own Sites and does not establish that a particular pattern increases completion or retention.

The linked [six ideals](../ux-principles.md) and six task patterns are our original synthesis. None of these sources supplies that exact taxonomy. The installed Sites building skill supplies local product constraints: working versus narrative surfaces, immediate access to the requested activity, scope control, and responsive behavior. We found no independently verified Sites-specific UX outcome study in this review; transfer from the sources below should be tested through real Site tasks.

No data, no matches, and failure mean different things. |

## Tradeoffs we should retain

- **Glanceability versus completeness:** a monitoring overview benefits from quick scanning; a planner or editor may properly scroll. Prioritize the first useful view, not a universal no-scroll rule.
- **Simplicity versus comparison:** secondary detail can be deferred, but hiding every attribute makes comparison harder. Keep decision-critical fields aligned and visible.
- **Action versus healthy quiet:** triage should emphasize unresolved work; monitoring may correctly report that nothing requires action. A dashboard does not owe the user a recommendation every time.
- **Creativity versus learned behavior:** vary composition and atmosphere; keep button, link, selection, save, and navigation behavior predictable.
- **Continuity versus infrastructure:** preserve work appropriate to the requested scope. Continuity is not permission to add accounts or remote storage.
- **Novelty versus evidence:** a recognizable pattern is not a failure. Judge whether it serves the task before requiring structural novelty.

## What would establish usefulness here

Run comparable briefs with and without this UX layer. Keep the content and capabilities constant. Observe whether people can identify the task, interpret scope/freshness, complete a representative action, recover from failure, and return from detail without losing context. Inspect both keyboard and narrow-screen flows. Record task completion, errors, and qualitative confusion; do not invent benchmark thresholds or claim improvement from screenshots alone.
